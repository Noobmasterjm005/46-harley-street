Wisdom tooth extraction

A wisdom tooth that has fully come through, and is not impacted, can often be removed without a surgical flap.

Impacted teeth may still need a surgical extraction. Dr James plans which approach fits the tooth.

What the treatment involves

  1. Assessment The position of the tooth, and whether it is impacted, decides the method.
  2. Non-surgical removal A fully erupted tooth is loosened and lifted out under local anaesthetic.
  3. Surgical removal An impacted tooth may need a small gum incision and, sometimes, the tooth divided.
  4. Aftercare A review is arranged if the socket needs to be checked.

Who it may suit

  • Non-surgical removal is limited to teeth that are fully erupted and not trapped in the jaw.
  • Pain, swelling, or food trapping around a wisdom tooth is a reason to be assessed.
  • Sedation can be added for surgical removal.
